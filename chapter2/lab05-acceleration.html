1 · The physics behind the motion

Acceleration measures how quickly velocity changes, not how fast an object moves. Both velocity and acceleration have signs because we choose positive x to the right.

aavg = (vf − vi)/(tf − ti)   [m/s²]

v(t) = v0 + at   x(t) = x0 + v0t + ½at²

When acceleration is constant, the slope of the velocity–time graph is a, and the acceleration–time graph is a horizontal line.

Remember: The car speeds up when v and a have the same sign, slows down when their signs are opposite, and momentarily stops when v = 0. Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down.

3 · Prediction challenges

Predict first, then use the simulator to investigate. Enter numerical answers without units; your answers are checked with a small rounding tolerance.

Challenge A · A car is braking

A car has initial velocity +12 m/s and reaches 0 m/s in 4 s under constant acceleration. What is its acceleration?

Reveal worked solution

a = (vf − vi)/Δt = (0 − 12)/4 = −3 m/s². The velocity is positive while the acceleration is negative, so the car slows down.

Challenge B · Predict the velocity

At t = 0, a car moves at +8 m/s with constant acceleration −2 m/s². Find its velocity after 3 s.

Reveal worked solution

v = v₀ + at = 8 + (−2)(3) = +2 m/s. It is still moving right but is slowing down.

Challenge C · Turning point

A car starts with v₀ = +6 m/s and acceleration a = −2 m/s². At what time does it stop momentarily before changing direction?

Reveal worked solution

At the turning point, v = 0. From 0 = 6 − 2t, we get t = 3 s. Immediately afterward v becomes negative, so the car reverses direction.

Challenge D · Negative acceleration

A car has v₀ = −6 m/s and a = −2 m/s². Is the car speeding up or slowing down at t = 2 s?

Reveal worked solution

v = −6 + (−2)(2) = −10 m/s. The speed is |v| = 10 m/s, greater than the initial speed 6 m/s. Because v and a are both negative, the car is speeding up to the left.

4 · Key ideas to take away

Slope of a velocity–time graph = acceleration. A positive slope gives positive acceleration; a negative slope gives negative acceleration; and a flat velocity graph means zero acceleration. For constant acceleration, the acceleration–time graph is horizontal.

Always check both signs. To decide whether the object speeds up or slows down, compare the signs of velocity and acceleration—not the sign of acceleration alone.

Model assumptions: straight-line motion; constant acceleration for each run; the clock starts at t = 0; x₀ = 0 m. Instantaneous velocity is the slope dx/dt; instantaneous acceleration is dv/dt. The car is shown on a fixed −120 m to +120 m coordinate scale.
