4 · Lecture example (page 26)

A ball is thrown vertically upward from the ground with initial speed 20 m/s. Take g = 10 m/s².

Maximum height: v² = v₀² − 2gΔy. At the top v = 0, so 0 = 20² − 2(10)H and H = 20 m.

Time up: 0 = 20 − 10t gives t = 2 s.

Return time: for an ideal launch and landing at the same height, ttotal = 2 × 2 = 4 s.

Notice: the ball's speed is zero for one instant at the apex, but gravity still acts downward. Velocity becomes negative on the descent.

5 · Student challenges

  1. Can you choose v₀ so that the ball reaches exactly 20 m when g = 10 m/s²?
  2. With v₀ = 30 m/s and g = 10 m/s², predict the maximum height, the time to reach it, and the total flight time.
  3. At t = 3 s for the lecture example, what are the ball's height and signed velocity? Is it rising or falling?
  4. Switch g from 10 to 9.8 m/s² while keeping the launch speed fixed. How does the maximum height change?
Reveal challenge solutions

1. v₀ = √(2gH) = √(400) = 20 m/s.

2. H = 30²/(2×10) = 45 m; tup = 30/10 = 3 s; flight time = 6 s.

3. y(3) = 20(3) − ½(10)(3²) = 15 m; v(3) = 20 − 10(3) = −10 m/s, falling.

4. H increases from 20 m to 400/19.6 ≈ 20.41 m because gravity is weaker.
