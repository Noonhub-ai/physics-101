Free-Fall Impact Challenge

Throw a ball downward from a building, predict when it will reach the ground, and connect the motion with height–time and velocity–time graphs. Based on Example 7, page 29 of your Chapter 2 slides.

1 · Predict before the release

In the lecture, a ball is thrown downward at 8 m/s from the top of a 30 m building. Take upward as positive and use g = 10 m/s².

Show the lecture's worked solution

Upward is positive: y₀ = 30 m, v₀ = −8 m/s, a = −10 m/s².

At ground impact, y = 0:

0 = 30 − 8t − 5t² → 5t² + 8t − 30 = 0.

t = (−8 + √(8² + 4·5·30)) / 10 = 1.7768 s ≈ 1.78 s.

v = −8 − 10(1.7768) = −25.768 m/s ≈ −25.8 m/s (downward).

The quadratic equation above includes the corrected constant term −30, required by the stated initial height.

Sign convention: upward = positive; release height y₀ = H; initial velocity v₀ = −u (u ≥ 0); acceleration a = −g.

Height: y(t) = H − ut − ½gt²

Velocity: v(t) = −u − gt

Impact velocity: vhit = −√(u² + 2gH)

4 · Student prediction challenges

A. A ball is released from rest from a 45 m building, with g = 10 m/s². What is the impact time?

Worked solution A0 = 45 − ½(10)t² → 5t² = 45 → t = √9 = 3 s.

B. A ball is thrown downward at 10 m/s from 40 m, with g = 10 m/s². Find the signed impact velocity.

Worked solution Bv² = v₀² + 2aΔy = 100 + 2(−10)(−40) = 900, so speed = 30 m/s. Motion is downward: v = −30 m/s.

C. With H = 30 m and downward speed u = 8 m/s, what is the ball's signed velocity after 1.00 s?

Worked solution Cv = −u − gt = −8 − (10)(1) = −18 m/s. The ball has not yet hit the ground at t = 1 s.
