Building Launch Simulator

Throw a ball upward from a building and investigate its journey from launch to ground impact. Based on Example 6 (pages 27–28) of your lecture slides.

1 · Predict before you launch

Take upward as positive and ground level as y = 0 m. In the lecture example, a ball is launched upward at 20 m/s from a 50 m building, with g = 10 m/s². What happens?

Show step-by-step lecture solution

Step 1: At maximum height, v = 0. From v = v₀ − gt: 0 = 20 − 10t, hence t = 2 s.

Step 2: Maximum rise above roof = v₀²/(2g) = 20²/(2 × 10) = 20 m. Therefore maximum height above ground = 50 + 20 = 70 m.

Step 3: Returning to roof level takes another 2 s. At t = 4 s, v = 20 − 10(4) = −20 m/s (downward).

Show the full worked solution for the current settings

If the initial velocity is zero or downward, there is no upward apex after launch; the maximum height is the initial building height. Roof return is only a later event when launch velocity is positive.

4 · Prediction challenges

Challenge A · Original lecture: How fast is the ball moving at t = 5 s, before impact? Give signed velocity.

Show solution A

v = v₀ − gt = 20 − 10 × 5 = −30 m/s, meaning downward. Since the ball hits the ground at about 5.74 s, t = 5 s is before impact.

Challenge B · Original lecture: What is the impact speed from a 50 m building when the ball is launched upward at 20 m/s and g = 10 m/s²?

Show solution B

v² = v₀² + 2aΔy = 20² + 2(−10)(0 − 50) = 1400. Thus impact speed = √1400 ≈ 37.42 m/s; signed impact velocity is approximately −37.42 m/s.

Challenge C · New situation: A ball is launched upward at 15 m/s from a 40 m building (g = 10 m/s²). Find its maximum height above ground.

Show solution C

Additional rise = v₀²/(2g) = 15²/20 = 11.25 m. Maximum height = 40 + 11.25 = 51.25 m.

5 · Student exploration missions

  1. With H = 50 m and v₀ = 20 m/s, predict when the ball returns to roof level. Verify the velocity is negative at that instant.
  2. Change H from 50 to 100 m without changing v₀. Does the maximum height above the roof change? Does the impact time change?
  3. At the apex, compare velocity with acceleration. Explain why the ball begins to fall.
  4. Choose a downward initial velocity. Explain why there is no later return to roof height.

Remember: Position and velocity depend on your chosen sign convention. Speed is always nonnegative, but velocity may be negative.
