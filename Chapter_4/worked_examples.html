Example 1 — Pulling a crate

A rope pulls a crate on a frictionless horizontal floor. Write the equations of motion.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
The forces are tension T, normal force N and weight mg.
Next step →
ΣFᵧ = N − mg = 0
Next step →
N = mg
Next step →
ΣFₓ = T = maₓ
Reveal answer
aₓ = T/m (rightward)

Example 3 — Hockey puck

A 0.3 kg puck moves on frictionless ice. F₁ = 5 N at 20° below +x and F₂ = 8 N at 60° above +x. Find the magnitude and direction of acceleration.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
F₁ₓ = 5 cos 20°; F₁ᵧ = −5 sin 20°
F₂ₓ = 8 cos 60°; F₂ᵧ = 8 sin 60°
Next step →
ΣFₓ = 8 cos 60° + 5 cos 20° = 8.70 N
Next step →
ΣFᵧ = 8 sin 60° − 5 sin 20° = 5.22 N
Next step →
aₓ = 8.70/0.3 = 29 m/s²
Next step →
aᵧ = 5.22/0.3 = 17.4 m/s²
Next step →
|a| = √(29² + 17.4²) ≈ 34 m/s²
Reveal answer
θ = tan⁻¹(17.4/29) ≈ 31° above +x

Example 4b — Rough horizontal surface

A horizontal force F = 100 N acts on a 2 kg block sliding on a rough surface with μₖ = 0.1. Find its acceleration.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
N = 2(10) = 20 N

fₖ = μₖN = 0.1(20) = 2 N

Example 5a — Smooth inclined plane

A 2 kg block slides down a smooth plane inclined at 37°. Find its acceleration.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
Choose +x down the slope and +y perpendicular outward.
Next step →
Weight components: mg sin θ along the slope; mg cos θ into the slope.
Next step →
ΣFᵧ = N − mg cos θ = 0
Next step →
ΣFₓ = mg sin θ = ma
Next step →
a = g sin 37°
Reveal answer
a ≈ 6 m/s² down the slope

Solution

Next step →
Fₓ = 10 cos 37° ≈ 8 N; Fᵧ = 10 sin 37° ≈ 6 N

Example 7 — Two hanging masses

Two masses hang over an ideal pulley: m₁ = 2 kg, m₂ = 3 kg. Find acceleration and tension.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
Choose upward positive for m₁ and downward positive for m₂.
Next step →
m₁: T − m₁g = m₁a
Next step →
m₂: m₂g − T = m₂a
Next step →
Add: m₂g − m₁g = (m₁ + m₂)a
Next step →
30 − 20 = 5a
Next step →
a = 2 m/s² (m₂ down, m₁ up)
Next step →
T − 20 = 2(2)
Reveal answer
T = 24 N

Example 9 — Traffic light

A 122 N traffic light hangs from three cables. The upper cables make 37° (left) and 53° (right) with the horizontal. Find T₁, T₂ and T₃.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
For the light: T₃ − 122 = 0 → T₃ = 122 N.
Next step →
For the knot: ΣFₓ = T₂ cos 53° − T₁ cos 37° = 0
Next step →
T₂ = T₁ cos 37° / cos 53°
Next step →
ΣFᵧ = T₁ sin 37° + T₂ sin 53° − 122 = 0
Next step →
T₁[sin 37° + (cos 37°/cos 53°) sin 53°] = 122
Next step →
T₁ ≈ 73.4 N
Reveal answer
T₂ ≈ 97.4 N; T₃ = 122 N

Example 10 — Critical angle

A block on a rough incline is just about to slip at θ꜀ = 15°. Find μₛ.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
At the threshold of slipping, a = 0 and fₛ = μₛN.
Next step →
ΣFᵧ = N − mg cos θ = 0
Next step →
N = mg cos θ
Next step →
ΣFₓ = mg sin θ − μₛN = 0
Next step →
mg sin θ = μₛ mg cos θ
Reveal answer
μₛ = tan 15° ≈ 0.27

Example 11 — Block against a wall

A force of 100 N at 37° above horizontal presses a 3 kg block against a vertical wall. Find the minimum coefficient of static friction for equilibrium.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
Fₓ = F cos θ; Fᵧ = F sin θ. Friction acts downward because Fᵧ > mg.
Next step →
ΣFₓ = F cos θ − N = 0
Next step →
N = F cos θ
Next step →
ΣFᵧ = F sin θ − mg − fₛ = 0
Next step →
At limiting equilibrium, fₛ = μₛN.
Next step →
μₛ = (F sin θ − mg)/(F cos θ)
Reveal answer
μₛ,min = [100 sin 37° − 30]/[100 cos 37°] ≈ 0.378

Example 12 — Contact force

Two blocks m₁ = 3 kg and m₂ = 2 kg touch on a smooth floor. A 50 N force pushes m₁ to the right. Find the contact force.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
m₁: F − P = m₁a
Next step →
m₂: P = m₂a
Next step →
Add: F = (m₁ + m₂)a
Next step →
a = 50/(3 + 2) = 10 m/s²
Next step →
P = m₂a = 2(10)
Reveal answer
P = 20 N

Example 16 — Pulley, friction and angled force

A block m₂ slides right on a rough horizontal surface and is connected over an ideal pulley to hanging mass m₁. A force F acts on m₂ at angle θ above horizontal. Derive the acceleration.

Free-body diagram

Solid arrows: forces · Dashed arrows: components or coordinate directions.

Solution

Next step →
m₂: ΣFᵧ = N + F sin θ − m₂g = 0
Next step →
N = m₂g − F sin θ (while contact is maintained)
Next step →
fₖ = μₖ(m₂g − F sin θ)
Next step →
m₁: T − m₁g = m₁a
Next step →
m₂: F cos θ − fₖ − T = m₂a
Next step →
Add: (m₁ + m₂)a = F cos θ − fₖ − m₁g
Next step →
a = [F(cos θ + μₖ sin θ) − (m₁ + μₖm₂)g]/(m₁ + m₂)
Reveal answer
Magnitude = |a|; a positive value indicates rightward acceleration of m₂.
