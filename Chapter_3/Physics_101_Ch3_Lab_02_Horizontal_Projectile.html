Horizontal Projectile from a Building

Launch a stone horizontally from a building. Observe why horizontal speed remains constant while vertical speed increases downward.

Assumptions: negligible air resistance; g = 10 m/s²; upward is positive; ground is y = 0.

Understand the physics

Initial conditions
θ = 0° → cos 0° = 1, sin 0° = 0
vₓ₀ = v₀ cos 0° = m/s
vᵧ₀ = v₀ sin 0° = 0 m/s
Horizontal motion (aₓ = 0)
vₓ = v₀ = m/s
Δx = v₀t = m
Vertical motion (aᵧ = −g)
vᵧ = −gt = m/s
Δy = −½gt² = m
Height now: y = H − ½gt² = m
Results at impact
T = √(2H/g) = s
R = v₀T = m
vᵧ,impact = −gT = m/s
Speed = √(vₓ² + vᵧ²) = m/s
Lecture Example 2 — pages 6–7

A stone travels 30 m horizontally after falling 50 m. Find its initial speed and impact speed using g = 10 m/s².

T = √(2 × 50 / 10) = √10 ≈ 3.16 s
v₀ = 30 / √10 ≈ 9.49 m/s
vᵧ,impact ≈ −31.6 m/s
Impact speed ≈ 33 m/s.

Press “Load lecture example” to reproduce this situation. The simulation uses unrounded values where possible, so displayed results may differ slightly.

Think, predict, test

1. Predict: if you double the horizontal speed but keep the building height the same, will the flight time change? Check with the controls.

2. Pause halfway through the fall. Why is the vertical velocity negative? What happens to vₓ?

3. Increase the building height without changing v₀. Compare the time of flight, range, and impact speed.

Practice challenge — check your answers

A ball is launched horizontally at 12 m/s from a 45 m building. Take g = 10 m/s². Find:

Show worked solution

Time: T = √(2H/g) = √(2 × 45 / 10) = 3.00 s.
Range: R = v₀T = 12 × 3 = 36.00 m.
Impact: vₓ = 12 m/s; vᵧ = −10 × 3 = −30 m/s.
Speed = √(12² + 30²) ≈ 32.31 m/s.
