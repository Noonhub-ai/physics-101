Projectile Launched at an Angle

Launch a ball from the ground and explore its horizontal range, maximum height, flight time, and velocity vectors.

Model: no air resistance; g = 10 m/s²; launch and landing at the same height.

Connect the motion to equations

Initial components
vₓ₀ = v₀ cos θ = m/s
vᵧ₀ = v₀ sin θ = m/s
Horizontal: aₓ = 0
vₓ = vₓ₀ = m/s
x = vₓ₀ t = m
Vertical: aᵧ = −g
vᵧ = vᵧ₀ − gt = m/s
y = vᵧ₀t − ½gt² = m
Same-height landing
T = 2vᵧ₀/g = s
hₘₐₓ = vᵧ₀²/(2g) = m
R = vₓ₀T = m

At the top: vᵧ = 0, but vₓ is still nonzero and acceleration is still downward.

Lecture Example 1

v₀ = 11 m/s, θ = 20°, g = 10 m/s². The slides round early to vₓ₀ = 10.3 m/s and vᵧ₀ = 3.8 m/s, giving T ≈ 0.76 s, R ≈ 7.8 m, h ≈ 0.72 m. This simulation keeps full precision until the final display, so values differ slightly.

Explore and predict

1. Keep v₀ fixed and compare angles 30°, 45°, and 60°. Which angle gives the greatest range? What do you notice about 30° and 60°?

2. Pause at the peak. What is vᵧ? Is the total velocity zero?

3. Change the initial speed but keep the angle constant. What happens to the range and maximum height?

Practice — check your answers

A projectile launches at 15 m/s and 30° with g = 10 m/s². Calculate:

Show worked solution

vₓ₀ = 15 cos30° ≈ 12.99 m/s; vᵧ₀ = 15 sin30° = 7.50 m/s.
T = 2(7.5)/10 = 1.50 s.
h = 7.5²/20 = 2.8125 m.
R = (12.99)(1.5) ≈ 19.49 m.
