Chapter 3 · Interactive Laboratory 03

Projectile Launched Between Buildings

Launch a stone upward at an angle from a tall building toward a lower rooftop. Study how an initial upward velocity and a negative vertical displacement determine the flight time.

Model: no air resistance · g = 10 m/s² · up is positive · building heights are measured from the same ground level.

Connect motion and equations

Initial components
vₓ₀ = v₀ cos θ = m/s
vᵧ₀ = v₀ sin θ = m/s
Horizontal (aₓ = 0)
vₓ = vₓ₀ = m/s
Δx = vₓ₀t = m
Vertical (aᵧ = −g)
Δy = y − y₀ = m
Δy = vᵧ₀t − ½gt²
vᵧ = vᵧ₀ − gt = m/s
At the landing rooftop
Δy = H₂ − H₁ = m
Positive root: T = s
Δx = vₓ₀T = m
Impact speed = √(vₓ² + vᵧ²) = m/s

Important: At maximum height, vᵧ = 0 but vₓ remains constant. The landing height is different from the launch height, so the same-height flight-time formula is not valid here.

Lecture Example 3 — pages 8–9

H₁ = 50 m, H₂ = 5 m, v₀ = 20 m/s, θ = 30°.

vₓ₀ = 17.32 m/s; vᵧ₀ = 10 m/s.
Δy = 5 − 50 = −45 m.
−45 = 10t − 5t² → t ≈ 4.16 s.
vᵧ,impact ≈ −31.62 m/s.
Impact speed ≈ 36.06 m/s.

Your slides round time to 4.2 s and report about 36.38 m/s using that rounded time. This lab retains full precision until displaying the final results.

Predict and investigate

1. Freeze the animation at the highest point. Is the stone stationary? What is its horizontal velocity?

2. Raise the landing rooftop while keeping the launch conditions fixed. Does the stone land earlier or later?

3. Keep the two building heights fixed. Increase the initial speed and predict the change in horizontal landing distance.

Practice challenge — check your answers

A stone is launched from a 45 m rooftop at 20 m/s and 30°, landing on a 15 m rooftop. Use g = 10 m/s².

Show worked solution

vₓ₀ = 20 cos 30° = 17.32 m/s; vᵧ₀ = 10 m/s.
Δy = 15 − 45 = −30 m.
−30 = 10t − 5t² → 5t² − 10t − 30 = 0.
t = (10 + √700)/10 ≈ 3.65 s.
R = (20 cos 30°)t ≈ 63.22 m.
vᵧ = 10 − 10t ≈ −26.46 m/s.
Impact speed = √[(17.32)² + (−26.46)²] ≈ 31.62 m/s.
